# Moral uncertainty and consciousness

## Epistemic uncertainty

Let C be a proposition about a system having morally relevant experience, and E observed evidence. Bayesian updating is:

\[
P(C\mid E)=\frac{P(E\mid C)P(C)}{P(E)}.
\]

**No validated likelihood model or consensus prior currently makes this formula a reliable numerical consciousness estimator for AI.** Assigning arbitrary values would create false precision.

## Model uncertainty

For theories M_j:

\[
P(C\mid E)=\sum_j P(C\mid E,M_j)P(M_j\mid E).
\]

This expression is meaningful only if the theories and their likelihoods are sufficiently specified. Some philosophical positions are not commensurable probabilistic models.

## Decision under moral uncertainty

A precautionary policy may minimize worst-case irreversible harm subject to protecting established human rights and avoiding unjustified anthropomorphism. The result depends on chosen ethical constraints and cannot be derived from probability theory alone.

## Epistemic guardrails

- Behavioral fluency is not direct evidence of phenomenal consciousness.
- Lack of a validated test does not prove absence of experience.
- Neither claimed sentience nor claimed non-sentience should automatically grant institutional authority.
- Record uncertainty and competing explanations explicitly.
