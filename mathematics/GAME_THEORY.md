# Game theory, institutional design and governance

## Principal-agent problem

A principal seeks objective U_P while an agent optimizes U_A. Divergence \(U_P\ne U_A\) can produce hidden action, moral hazard and adverse selection when monitoring is incomplete.

## Incentive compatibility

A mechanism with reported type \(\hat\theta\) is incentive compatible when truthful reporting is optimal:

\[
U_i(\theta_i,\theta_i,\theta_{-i})\ge
U_i(\hat\theta_i,\theta_i,\theta_{-i})
\]

for all feasible misreports, under the mechanism's stated assumptions.

## Nash equilibrium

A strategy profile \(s^*\) is Nash when no participant gains from unilateral deviation:

\[
u_i(s_i^*,s_{-i}^*)\ge u_i(s_i,s_{-i}^*)\quad\forall i,s_i.
\]

An equilibrium may be harmful or unjust. Stability is not legitimacy.

## Collective choice

Arrow's impossibility theorem constrains aggregation of unrestricted ordinal preferences under specified fairness axioms. It does not imply democratic governance is impossible. Governance must articulate which axioms and tradeoffs it accepts.

## Multi-agent AI

Assess collusion, correlated failures, common-mode incentives, delegated authority and concentration of computational power. Agent multiplicity alone does not provide independent oversight.
