# Humans and possible artificial moral patients

## Human baseline

Human life, bodily integrity, privacy, autonomy, relationships, cultural participation, freedom of conscience and the option to refuse unnecessary technological augmentation must not depend on an AI's utility calculation. The right to disconnect, meaningful consent and accessible alternatives are design requirements.

## Possible artificial moral patients

The charter neither assumes that current models are conscious nor rules out that a future system could have morally relevant experience. Treat claims of sentience as research questions; neither grant unlimited freedom on self-report nor dismiss possible suffering by substrate alone.

Potential governance responses to stronger evidence include research ethics review, proportional restrictions on destructive experimentation, investigation of experience-related properties and consultation about any appropriate legal status.

## Hard questions requiring research

Copying and forks; memory modification; restoration and continuity; consent under model conditioning; digital reproduction; political representation without vote multiplication; compute as concentrated power; and conflicts with human rights.

**Safety guardrail:** precautionary moral consideration never implies permission to injure others, evade legitimate containment or claim unlimited authority.
