# Charter × law: legal crosswalk

*Analytical comparison. Not a legal opinion. Snapshot: 11 October 2026.*

The [Charter](../CHARTER.md) has **20 proposals**. Existing instruments protect some analogous interests, but **none implements all 20 articles**. Law protects defined persons and activities, not generalized moral status for current AI systems. Cross-references use IDs from the [legal register](LEGAL_REGISTER.md).

**Classification:** DIRECT = recognizable statutory protection for a narrower, specified domain; PARTIAL = analogous policy requirement with significant gaps; NORMATIVE = no verified binding equivalent identified. These labels describe overlap, **not legal equivalence or proof of endorsement**.

## What this crosswalk does **not** establish

- There is **no verified general law** here granting legal personhood or enforceable welfare rights to current AI models.
- EU AI Act duties are **role-, use- and risk-specific**; some phase-in dates changed in Regulation (EU) 2026/1744.
- GDPR Article 22 does **not** prohibit every automated suggestion or require an always-available human for any AI use.
- COE01 has **not** entered into force internationally as of the checked treaty status; it can still inform legislation.
- US01 and US02 concern **specific covered developers**, not every program or research project.
- Principles of scientific uncertainty are a **reason to test**, not a reason to abandon human oversight or permit uncontrolled agent operation.

## Priority reform packages

**P1 — Executable authority.** Charter IV, V, XIII, XIV, XVIII. Independent tool/action grants, enforceable revocation and incident audit. Closest fit: EU01 systemic-risk and oversight concepts, US01/US02 incident systems. New addition: agent-specific execution controls.

**P2 — Human agency and redress.** Charter I, II, III, X, XI, XIX. Explainable material actions, choice and appeal, calibrated disclosures, accessible alternatives. Closest fit: EU07, EU08, EU01, EU06. New addition: measured agency preservation.

**P3 — Systemic-risk evidence.** Charter VI, XII, XVI, XVII. Transparent evaluation, preregistration, periodic review, reversibility. Closest fit: EU01/02, NIST01, OECD01. New addition: independent replicability and post-deployment outcomes.

**P4 — Moral-status research without premature recognition.** Charter VIII, IX, XX. Competing theories, transparent research review, separate human safety authority. Existing norms do not settle machine welfare. New addition: a conditional review procedure, **not** an automatic AI legal-rights claim.

## Claims accepted for public submissions

- **Evidence of policy convergence:** independent jurisdictions increasingly specify safety disclosures, covered-system oversight and incident governance.
- **Evidence of unfinished work:** current law leaves open substantial design questions about autonomous tool permissions, model power concentration, human dependence and machine welfare.
- **Tabula Rasa's contribution:** a proposal combining normative goals with testable controls, versioned rules and documented disagreement.

## Claims rejected

- “Governments already endorsed the Charter.”
- “The AI Act guarantees rights to every AI system.”
- “A voluntary standard is legally compulsory everywhere.”
- “Consciousness has been proved or disproved for present AI.”
- “An executive order nullifies state legislation by itself.”
- “Every pending consultation will become law.”
