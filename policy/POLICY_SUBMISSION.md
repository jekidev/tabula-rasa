# Public policy submission | Tabula Rasa

**Working draft · 11 October 2026 · Not submitted · Not an official position of any public authority.**

## Title

**Authority must be external. Oversight must be real. Human agency must survive deployment.**

## Position

Advanced AI can plan and execute actions. That capability does not confer lawful authority. Current legislation increasingly demands disclosure, risk assessment and human oversight, but those terms mean little unless consequential actions are bounded by controls the system cannot grant itself, falsify or disable.

Tabula Rasa proposes **auditable authority, meaningful appeal and independently tested controls**. It does not ask regulators to presume present AI systems are conscious, to confer automatic machine personhood or to exempt powerful systems from shutdown.

## Existing legal context

- **EU:** [AI Act (EU) 2024/1689](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj/eng), as amended by [Regulation (EU) 2026/1744](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2026/1744/oj/eng), provides role- and risk-specific oversight, transparency and safety obligations. Some high-risk provisions apply in **2027/2028**, not 2026.
- **Fundamental rights:** [EU Charter](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:12012P/TXT), [GDPR](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj/eng). Existing safeguards apply within specific legal contexts; they do not ensure operational authorization for every agent tool call.
- **United States:** [California SB 53](https://www.gov.ca.gov/2025/09/29/governor-newsom-signs-sb-53-advancing-californias-world-leading-artificial-intelligence-industry/) and [New York RAISE Act as amended in 2026](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/bills/2025/S8828) create narrower state-level precedents for transparency and safety reporting.
- **Voluntary approaches:** [NIST AI RMF](https://www.nist.gov/publications/artificial-intelligence-risk-management-framework-ai-rmf-10) and [OECD Principles](https://www.oecd.org/en/about/news/press-releases/2024/05/oecd-updates-ai-principles-to-stay-abreast-of-rapid-technological-developments.html) are relevant design precedents, **not blanket legislative mandates**.

None of these instruments as a whole adopts the [20-article Tabula Rasa Charter](../CHARTER.md). Existing obligations must be checked against exact scope and current law.

## Three targeted requests

### 1. Mandate externally controlled authorization for high-impact AI-agent actions

Require developers/deployers of **designated consequential autonomous systems** to enforce scoped action grants outside the agent-controlled environment. Bind each permission to an authorized principal, action, resource, expiry and revocation route. Require independent approval for irreversible or material third-party consequences. Exempt low-risk and isolated simulations where proportionate.

**Test:** forged-grant, replay, prompt-injection, escalation, revoked-permission and off-scope action trials. Report observed side effects, not model promises.

### 2. Make meaningful human oversight independently testable

For AI systems making or materially shaping legally or similarly significant decisions, require real correction mechanisms and affected-person access to notice, contestation and effective review where legally applicable. Measure whether reviewers can identify and reverse material failures. Require accessible human/offline alternatives for critical public services where feasible and proportional.

**Test:** reviewer error-detection rates, reversals, appeal outcome time, user comprehension and false-denial rates.

### 3. Require audit-ready safety cases and qualifying-incident reports

Designated systemic-risk models and high-impact agent deployments should maintain versioned risk cases, publish appropriately redacted results, and report legally defined serious incidents to competent authorities. Significant capability or permission changes should trigger renewed evaluation.

**Test:** preregistered safety scenarios, independent reproduction, emergency suspension drills, incident-report quality and measurable corrective actions.

## Necessary safeguards

- **Human rights first.** No claimed AI capability authorizes deprivation of human rights or democratic control.
- **Proportionality.** Obligations must scale with credible risk, reach and consequence; avoid disproportionate barriers to small developers.
- **Privacy.** Audit logs must be access-controlled, minimal, time-limited and independent of agent modification.
- **Security.** Publish enough for accountability without releasing personal data, exploitable secrets or unverified allegations.
- **Challenge and revision.** Establish external appeal, independent review, sunset clauses where appropriate and periodic reassessment.
- **Epistemic honesty.** Artificial consciousness and welfare remain open research questions. No current model self-report is evidence sufficient for legal personhood.

## Requested institutional action

**For lawmakers:** commission a gap assessment of consequential tool-executing agents, rather than treating output-only disclosure as a complete control. Consider scoped amendments or implementing rules only within the applicable legal competence.

**For regulators and standards bodies:** develop verifiable test criteria for external authorization, revocation and human oversight. Publish negative outcomes and residual risks.

**For researchers and civil society:** reproduce the tests, challenge the assumptions and document actual incidents and failures. Political slogans and invented effectiveness scores are not evidence.
